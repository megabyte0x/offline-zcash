# Prior art and settlement/double-spend architecture for offline value transfer (for a Zcash Ironwood-over-Logos-mesh design)

Scope: real projects that moved crypto or digital value without internet (mesh, LoRa, HF, satellite, SMS/USSD, sneakernet), and the architecture patterns behind them. For each pattern the notes cover trust assumptions, the double-spend window, the finality point, what an offline recipient can verify, privacy, and failure modes. The last two sections assess fit with a shielded Zcash (Ironwood) pool on testnet. Status is as of 2026-10-24. "Inference" means my reasoning, not a sourced fact.

---

## 1. Real non-Zcash projects: how they worked, current status, what failed

### Takeaway
Every working "Bitcoin without internet" project is a transport for an already-signed transaction (store-and-forward to an internet gateway). None of them solves double-spending offline. Finality always comes later, when a gateway broadcasts and the transaction is mined. The ones that feel "instant" (Machankura, Cashu/Fedimint over bitchat) are custodial, and the custodian or mint is the double-spend authority. Most of the 2018–2020 hardware efforts (goTenna/TxTenna, Locha/Turpial) are dead or stalled. The current wave is Meshtastic/LoRa hobby bridges and hackathon projects. Blockstream Satellite (receive-only) is still running.

### Cited Findings

**goTenna + Samourai TxTenna (2018–2019): dead**
- In 2018 Samourai Wallet built TxTenna, an Android app on goTenna's SDK. The user signs a normal bitcoin transaction offline in Samourai Wallet. TxTenna sends it over a paired goTenna Mesh radio, other goTenna devices relay it, and an internet-connected goTenna node running TxTenna forwards it to the Bitcoin network. In October 2018 goTenna reported confirmed transactions on its testnet, including a 150 m two-device test. — [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/culture/samourai-and-gotenna-enable-bitcoin-transactions-without-internet-access); [CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/tecnologia/gotenna-samourai-confirman-transacciones-fuera-linea-testnet-bitcoin)
- The txTenna repo calls itself "a proof-of-concept Android app developed by Samourai" (Mule.Tools initiative) for "censorship resistance". It sends over SMS or goTenna mesh. The hex transaction is split into JSON segments with fields `s` (segment count), `h` (tx hash), `i` (payload ID), `c` (sequence), `t` (data) and `n` (network; `t` = TestNet3). Optional Z85 encoding gives "40% less data". The gateway broadcasts "via the Samourai node pushTx". Known problems listed: "The occasional dropped SMS" / "dropped data packet". The README describes no confirmation path back to the sender. — [github.com/remyers/txTenna](https://github.com/remyers/txTenna)
- On 11 May 2019 Blockstream and goTenna announced `txtenna-python` (by goTenna's Richard Myers). An off-grid full node gets blockchain data from Blockstream Satellite "without ever needing to send a packet onto the internet" and sends signed transactions out over goTenna mesh. Any node with internet relays them to the Bitcoin P2P network. Satellite API data can also be re-relayed over the mesh to users without a dish. — [Blockstream blog](https://blog.blockstream.com/en-gotenna-satellite-api-integration)
- goTenna's own commentary: the satellite side carries only Bitcoin network data, so offline users cannot add transactions to the ledger through it. — [goTenna blog](https://gotenna.com/blogs/blockchain/bitcoin-btc-can-still-be-censored-but-there-is-a-way-out)
- Status: Forterra (a defense/ground-autonomy company) announced it acquired goTenna on 9 Oct 2025 and positioned it for military, law-enforcement and emergency users. — [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/?p=66822); [Forterra](https://thelastmile.forterra.com/forterras-gotenna-acquisition-paving-the-way-for-a-new-era-of-autonomous-systems-and-operator-safety/). One aggregator says "goTenna discontinued all consumer products in 2024" and that TxTenna "is now defunct". The same page dates the acquisition to Aug 2025, which conflicts with Forterra's Oct 2025 announcement. Treat the consumer-discontinuation claim as unverified. — [spark.money comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)
- Samourai Wallet's founders pleaded guilty in July 2025 to conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business. They were sentenced on 6 Nov 2025 (Rodriguez, 5 years plus a $250k fine) and 19 Nov 2025 (Hill, 4 years). — [US DOJ SDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/founders-samourai-wallet-cryptocurrency-mixing-service-sentenced-five-and-four-years)

**Lot49 (goTenna/Global Mesh Labs, June 2019): research only**
- A white paper for a Lightning-derived payment-channel network adapted to low-bandwidth ad hoc mesh. It used simplex channel updates and signature aggregation to cut the data exchanged between off-grid nodes and the data sent through internet gateways for settlement. Channels are formed between nodes in radio range, and micropayments reward relaying. — [github.com/remyers/lot49](https://github.com/remyers/lot49); [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/culture/mobile-mesh-network-users-get-new-incentives-via-lightning)
- A later review judged Lot49 to have too many requirements to be feasible, citing its dependence on Schnorr signatures and SIGHASH_NOINPUT. — [arXiv 2304.14559](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.14559)

**Blockstream Satellite: active, receive-only, Bitcoin-only**
- GEO satellites broadcast the Bitcoin blockchain (Satellite 2.0, DVB-S2). Users with a dish can run a full node that validates blocks offline. The service is receive-only for end users. Transaction broadcast needs another channel, and the paid Satellite API (data uplink paid by Lightning invoice) needs internet. — [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison); [Blockstream satellite queue](https://blockstream.com/satellite-queue/); [Satellite API wiki](https://github.com/Blockstream/satellite-api/wiki)
- Still maintained. v2.5.0 (31 Dec 2024) changed the Telstar 11N Africa/Europe frequencies and added the `blocksat-gui` app. v2.5.1 (8 May 2025) updated the Telstar 18V C-band frequency. No release mentions discontinuation. — [GitHub releases](https://github.com/Blockstream/satellite/releases)

**Meshtastic / LoRa Bitcoin bridges (2023–2025): active hobby and hackathon projects**
- `MeshtasticBitcoinCore_Bridge` (BTCtoolshed) is a Python script on a machine with Bitcoin Core plus a USB Meshtastic node. The Meshtastic app buffer is 228 bytes and a raw tx hex "may be more than 1000 characters", so the user sends segments of 200 characters or fewer prefixed with `+`, in order, and ends with `==`. The bridge acks each segment with "recvd.." and the running length, then calls bitcoin-cli to broadcast ("FIN!"). `-mempool-` returns a fee estimate and `-clear-` resets. The README says nothing about encryption. — [GitHub](https://github.com/BTCtoolshed/MeshtasticBitcoinCore_Bridge)
- BTC Mesh (eddieoz) is a similar relay: it reassembles chunked tx hex from Meshtastic DMs, validates it and submits to Bitcoin Core RPC. At LongFast (~1.07 kbps) a simple tx takes about 1–2 s on air. Meshtastic presets range from 21.88 kbps (Short Turbo) down to 0.18 kbps (Long Slow), with 3–10 km range, and "LoRa mesh cannot sync the blockchain". — [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)
- Darkwire (Bitcoin 2025 hackathon, pseudonymous dev "cyber") is a custom LoRa mesh on Arduino UNO plus LoRa transceivers. Packets hop until they reach an internet-connected "exit" node, which broadcasts the signed tx. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/322192/project-send-bitcoin-without-internet-access)
- Solana analogue: AnonMesh reportedly relays encrypted Solana transactions over Bluetooth/LoRa (Meshtastic) until a connected node settles them. Caveat: this attribution comes from search-result summaries and I did not fetch the page. — [Triton One OSS report Jan 2026](https://blog.triton.one/oss-report-january-2026/)

**Locha Mesh / Turpial (Venezuela): dead**
- Monero CCS funded 150 XMR for docs and a Turpial radio-mesh demo. All milestones were marked complete, the last on 20 July 2020. — [Monero CCS](https://ccs.getmonero.org/proposals/randybrito-locha-mesh.html)
- The company later stopped work on both the Turpial consumer device and the dev kits. Donors were offered a 56% USD refund or a dev kit. — [CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/tecnologia/locha-adios-antes-terminar-equipos-enviar-bitcoin-sin-internet) (the page returned 403 to me; facts come from the search snippet). An aggregator lists it as "Stalled… no major updates since 2023" (custom CC1312R sub-GHz plus ESP32 board, ~$200, 7.16 km open-terrain test). — [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)

**HF radio (JS8Call, 2019): one-off demos**
- Feb 2019: Rodolfo Novak (Coinkite) sent BTC from Toronto to Michigan on 7.077 MHz (40 m) using JS8Call and a pre-configured brain wallet. Nick Szabo commented that it had crossed a border relying only on the ionosphere. Sources disagree on the mechanics: some say a brain wallet, others that a private key was handed over and the recipient swept it. Either way it was a bearer-key transfer, not a signed tx to the recipient's address. — [Bitcoinist](https://bitcoinist.com/bitcoin-sent-ham-radio/amp/); [dxzone](https://www.dxzone.com/bitcoin-sent-through-amateur-radio-40-meters-band); [techgenyz](https://techgenyz.com/transmission-of-bitcoin-without-internet-or-satellite/)
- March 2019: a Lightning invoice was sent as 21 JS8 messages on 20 m, Toronto to San Francisco. Commenters raised amateur-radio rules on commercial and encrypted traffic, and Novak noted encryption isn't allowed unless the cipher is public. — [rtl-sdr.com](https://www.rtl-sdr.com/transferring-files-via-the-blockstream-satellite-with-lightning-network-payments-and-rtl-sdr-transacting-bitcoin-over-hf/); [techgenyz](https://techgenyz.com/transmission-of-bitcoin-without-internet-or-satellite/)
- JS8Call throughput is about 0.01–0.05 kbps, rated "Experimental", and a licence is needed in most jurisdictions. — [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)

**SMS / USSD**
- SMS relays: PonyDirect (Samourai/Mule.Tools) splits a signed tx into SMS segments sent to a relay. The relay reconstitutes the hex, validates the hash and broadcasts via Samourai's pushTx. PonyRelay (Stadicus) is an alpha Python bridge to your own node. Pavol Rusnak documented a virtual-number webhook gateway feeding a Flask server. CoinText offered custodial SMS payments for BCH. — [PonyDirect](https://github.com/MuleTools/PonyDirect); [PonyRelay](https://github.com/Stadicus/PonyRelay); [rusnak.io](https://rusnak.io/how-to-push-bitcoin-transactions-via-sms/); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2018/10/140079-cointext-introduces-buy-command-feature-to-pay-for-bitpay-invoices-through-sms-messages/)
- Machankura (active): a feature phone dials a USSD code and the session goes through the aggregator Africa's Talking to Machankura's servers. Menus are limited to 160 characters per prompt and 20 s per response. Each phone number maps to a custodial Lightning wallet with a Lightning address (e.g., phonenumber@8333.mobi). Fee is 1%. It operates in 10 African countries and reports more than 19 BTC of cumulative volume (company figure). — [TechCabal (Mar 2026)](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/04/machankuras-solution-for-crypto-transactions/); [Mariblock](https://www.mariblock.com/stories/south-africa-based-ussd-bitcoin-transfer-service-launches-in-ghana). An aggregator cites ~39K users and says "Machankura 2.0" (a SIM Java Card applet for self-custody) is in development. — [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)

**Reticulum / LXMF and Nostr**
- LXMF Propagation Nodes store encrypted messages for offline recipients, and LXMF is designed to work over packet radio and LoRa. I found no documented Bitcoin or crypto tx relay built on it. A vendor only asserts that it can carry "a signed transaction". — [LXMF GitHub](https://github.com/markqvist/lxmf); [D-Central](https://d-central.tech/reticulum-network-guide/)
- I found no Nostr NIP or event kind for relaying blockchain transactions. Nostr store-and-forward needs relays that are reachable online. — [satscryption analysis](https://satscryption.substack.com/p/decoding-nostr); [W3C public-nostr list](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-nostr/2025Mar/0007.html)

**Ecash over mesh (Cashu / Fedimint / bitchat, 2025–2026): active, custodial**
- Calle (Cashu's creator) built bitchat's Android app, which now has a native Cashu wallet. Calle filmed two phones exchanging sats over the bitchat Bluetooth mesh with no internet. Final settlement happens only when the holder reconnects and redeems with the mint. — [btcpp insider](https://insider.btcpp.dev/p/knock-knock-its-cashu-bitch); [Forbes via mirror](https://drss.io/reader/npub19ma2w9dmk3kat0nt0k5dwuqzvmg3va9ezwup0zkakhpwv0vcwvcsg8axkl/https:%2F%2Fwww.forbes.com%2Fsites%2Fdavidbirnbaum%2F2025%2F07%2F31%2Fcan-bitchat-build-a-free-internet-with-bitcoin%2F-1754680224)
- Cashu's double-spend check happens only at redemption or swap at the mint. NUT-12 DLEQ proofs let a recipient check the mint's signature offline, which shows the token is genuine but not that it is unspent. "There is also no one else that can tell if it's a double spend besides the mint." — [Delving Bitcoin](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/building-intuition-for-the-cashu-blind-signature-scheme/506/2); [spark.money glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/cashu)
- NUT-11 P2PK locks proofs to a recipient pubkey ("The spending condition is enforced by the mint"). Optional `locktime` plus `refund` keys add a second spending path after expiry. If there is no refund tag, an expired lock becomes "spendable without a witness signature". `n_sigs` gives multisig. — [Cashu NUT-11](https://cashubtc.github.io/nuts/11/)
- Fedimint has a "trust race condition": a malicious sender who gets online first can redeem the ecash, and the receiver's later redemption fails. — [Delving Bitcoin Fedimint overview (mirror)](https://mirror.b10c.me/delvingbitcoin-org/2023-09-29-fedimint-overview-and-fedipool-theorizing-id110.html)

### Inferences
- Pattern of failure: projects tied to one company's hardware or gateway (goTenna Mesh, Samourai pushTx, Locha Turpial) died when the company pivoted, was acquired, or was prosecuted. Survivors use commodity hardware (Meshtastic/LoRa) and let anyone run the gateway with their own node. A Zcash design should allow any basecamp with backhaul to act as a gateway with its own Zebra/zcashd/lightwalletd. It should not depend on one operator's relay.
- txTenna had no confirmation path back to the sender. In practice the off-grid sender never learned whether or when the transaction was mined, unless they also had satellite receive (`txtenna-python`). A mesh design needs an explicit receipt/confirmation message flowing back (tx mined at height H, with a header proof if possible).
- Segmentation and in-order delivery with manual retransmission (`-clear-`, dropped SMS) were the main usability failures. Logos Messaging's store and reliability layers should absorb this (that is the other researchers' scope).
- Regulatory lesson (inference): gateway and relay operators and custodial ledgers (Machankura, mints) are the legal choke points. Samourai's prosecution concerned its mixing service, not txTenna, but the relay path depended on Samourai's servers anyway.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation of when goTenna Mesh (consumer) was end-of-lifed, or whether the TxTenna app/gateway still functions. Probably not, given Samourai's shutdown, but unverified.
- The current maintenance status of PonyDirect/PonyRelay, BTC Mesh (eddieoz) and Darkwire after 2025 was not checked.
- The exact Locha Mesh shutdown date was not retrieved (CriptoNoticias returned 403).
- No documented, working Bitcoin relay over Reticulum/LXMF or Nostr was found.

## 2. Zcash-specific offline / mesh / SMS / Nostr efforts

### Takeaway
I found no shipped system that moves ZEC over Meshtastic/LoRa, SMS, USSD, Nostr or any mesh. The only mesh-adjacent ZCG application (ZK-LoRa, June 2026) was rejected, and it would only have sent proofs over LoRa anyway. The Zcash building blocks that matter are: ZIP 324 (URI-encapsulated bearer payments; Draft, no wallet implementation found), PCZT (ZIP 374; split construction, proving and signing, already used for air-gapped Keystone/Ledger signing), FROST for spend-authorization multisig (ZIP 312 Draft; ZF frost-core, frostd and frost-client audited; Zkool ships FROST multisig), ZIP 221 FlyClient header commitments, and the new Ironwood pool (NU6.3, mainnet 28 Jul 2026). Zcash core engineers have publicly said offline-to-offline payments cannot be made safe against double-spending without trust.

### Cited Findings
- No working ZEC-over-Meshtastic/LoRa project surfaced. The only Zcash hit was a grant application. — [search; Zcash forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/grant-application-zk-lora-zero-knowledge-privacy-layer-for-ai-to-ai-mesh-networks/56463)
- **ZK-LoRa (ZCG application, posted 30 Jun 2026, rejected 7 Jul 2026).** It proposed a LoRa/IoT mesh carrying a 128-byte compressed Groth16 proof inside 255-byte LoRa packets, with "Settlement lands fully shielded on-chain". Zcash SDK integration was a future milestone, so no Zcash transactions would cross LoRa. It requested $24,500, but the listed milestones total $17,000. — [Zcash forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/grant-application-zk-lora-zero-knowledge-privacy-layer-for-ai-to-ai-mesh-networks/56463)
- **Forum thread "How to pay offline with zcash in any situation" (24 Jan 2025).** HHIROSHIMA proposed Zashi "offline addresses" funded online and spent offline via Bluetooth. nuttycom replied: "The primary problem with this idea is that it's vulnerable to double-spending attacks" and "Preventing double-spending is literally the only problem that blockchains solve." He said he would not build a protocol inviting double-spend fraud, and that the recipient bears the risk because a malicious Zashi build can't be prevented. aprojection said offline-to-offline "will always be exploitable" and suggested paper wallets swept later. james_katz said an offline wallet would need a "temporary blockchain" and that the recipient must trust the payer's software. HHIROSHIMA retreated to a secure-element hardware idea. No solution was adopted. — [Zcash forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/how-to-pay-offline-with-zcash-in-any-situation/50167)
- **ZIP 324, URI-Encapsulated Payments.** Draft, created 2019-07-17. Owners: Jack Grigg, Daira-Emma Hopwood, Kris Nuttycombe. Original authors include Ian Miers and Eran Tromer. The sender funds an ephemeral note derived from a 256-bit `key` carried in the URI fragment (`https://pay.withzcash.com:65536/payment/v1#…`, port 65536 deliberately invalid). "Anyone who learns the URI can accept this payment." The recipient's wallet does a blockchain lookup to confirm the notes exist and are unspent (Ready-to-finalize / Invalid / Pending), then finalizes by sweeping to its own address. 10 confirmations is suggested. The sender "is capable of cancelling the payment, by themselves finalizing the payment into their own wallet" and "MUST appropriately handle the race condition where the recipient initiated finalization concurrently". "Both wallets MUST NOT convey the payment as 'finalized'" before enough confirmations. Interception means theft. Ephemeral keys derive from the sender's seed (ZIP 32 path `m_Sapling/324'/coin_type'/payment_index'`), so the sender can always reclaim. The note derivation is Sapling-based, with TODO/placeholder (`0xFIXME`) parts. — [ZIP 324](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0324)
- I found no evidence that any wallet implements ZIP 324. Searches surface only ZIP 321 payment-request URIs (Kotlin/Swift libraries audited by Least Authority, 11 Jun 2025). — [Least Authority](https://leastauthority.com/blog/audit-of-zcash-kotlin-and-swift-payment-uri-prototypes/); [ZecHub](https://zechub.wiki/using-zcash/payment-request-uris)
- **PCZT.** The `pczt` crate splits Zcash tx creation across separate entities, with roles analogous to BIP 174/370 plus Zcash-specific ones. The API is specified in ZIP 374 (draft). — [docs.rs pczt](https://docs.rs/pczt/). Ledger's DMK signs Orchard spend-authorization signatures over a host-built PCZT, and the binding signature and assembly are done on the host. — [Ledger developers](https://developers.ledger.com/docs/device-interaction/dmk-ts/references/signers/zcash). ECC shipped Keystone hardware integration with Zashi around end of 2024. Least Authority's Keystone audit was completed 20 Mar 2025. Zashi has since been renamed Zodl, which supports Keystone Pro 3 with "Cypherpunk" firmware. — [ZecHub newsletter](https://zechub.substack.com/p/zcash-shielded-news-volume-2); [Least Authority](https://leastauthority.com/blog/audit-of-keystone-hardware-wallet-for-zcash/); [Zodl support](https://support.zodl.com/article/33-connecting-a-keystone-wallet)
- **FROST for Zcash.** RFC 9591 (June 2024) specifies FROST, with ZF staff among the authors. — [RFC 9591](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9591.html). ZIP 312 "FROST for Spend Authorization Multisignatures" (Draft; Gouvea, Komlo, Connolly) defines FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) for Orchard and FROST(Jubjub, BLAKE2b-512) for Sapling, with re-randomization so signatures are unlinkable. Only `ask` is threshold-shared. The randomizer must go "over a confidential and authenticated channel". A rogue Coordinator "will be able to break unlinkability and privacy". Signers "MUST check that the given SIGHASH matches the data sent from the Coordinator". A malicious Coordinator should not be able to create signed transactions without approval from `MIN_PARTICIPANTS`. — [ZIP 312](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0312)
- ZF's FROST book: only shares of the spend authorization key exist. For Orchard, `nk` and `rivk` are derived or generated outside FROST, and the backup needs the full viewing key (`ak`, `nk`, `rivk`). A seed phrase alone can't restore a FROST wallet. "Communication is described as the biggest challenge". The book suggests Orchard alone may be simpler to support. Example use: 2-of-3 shared custody (user, service, cloud backup). — [frost.zfnd.org/zcash](https://frost.zfnd.org/zcash.html); [technical details](https://frost.zfnd.org/zcash/technical-details.html)
- ZF built `frostd` (server) and `frost-client` (CLI) as wallet-integration references. Messages are end-to-end encrypted, so frostd needn't be trusted. Least Authority audited the server and client (final report 29 Apr 2025). FROST v3.0.0-rc.0 (reported Feb 2026) made cheater detection the default. — [ZecHub Vol.32](https://zechub.substack.com/p/zcash-shielded-news-vol32); [Least Authority](https://leastauthority.com/blog/zcash-frost-demo/); [frost-rerandomized docs](https://docs.rs/frost-rerandomized)
- Zkool (hhanh00) advertises "shielded multi signature accounts using FROST" and supports Orchard. — [zkool2 wiki](https://github.com/hhanh00/zkool2/wiki); [ZecHub wallets](https://zechub.wiki/using-zcash/wallets)
- **Ironwood.** NU6.3 activated on mainnet 28 Jul 2026 at height 3,428,143 after Taylor Hornby (Shielded Labs) found an Orchard circuit flaw that could in theory allow undetected counterfeiting. Ironwood reuses the fixed Orchard circuit and Halo 2 "with some minor modifications" and "has been formally verified". Orchard became withdrawal-only behind a "turnstile". The upgrade adds ZIP 2005 quantum-recoverable notes. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/409934/zcash-ironwood-upgrade-launching-new-shielded-pool-after-orchard-vulnerability). Aggregated reporting says Ironwood has its own note commitment tree, nullifier set and value pool, and transaction format v6. — [Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/07/29/zcash-ironwood-upgrade-security/); [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/zcash-ironwood-pool-80m-first-day-2/) (not verified against a primary spec)
- **ZIP 221 (FlyClient, Final).** Headers commit (via `hashLightClientRoot`, later `hashBlockCommitments`) to an MMR of chain history. Light clients can verify chain validity and block inclusion with logarithmically many headers, "correct with high probability". MMR leaves commit to Sapling/Orchard note commitment roots, but a nullifier-set commitment was considered and **not** included. "The primary cost of a light client is transmission of Equihash solution information in block headers." — [ZIP 221](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0221)
- A serialized Zcash block header is 1,487 bytes. The Equihash solution is 1,344 bytes (mainnet/testnet). — [Zebra BLOCK_HEADER_LENGTH](https://doc-internal.zebra.zfnd.org/zebra_chain/block/header/constant.BLOCK_HEADER_LENGTH.html); [Zebra Solution](https://doc-internal.zebra.zfnd.org/zebra_chain/work/equihash/enum.Solution.html)
- **Tachyon (in development).** Proof-carrying data, oblivious sync, changed nullifier derivation, and out-of-band delivery of note secrets. Bowe and Miers' "A Note on Notes" discusses unbounded nullifier-set growth. A Sept 2026 report says Tachyon's recursive proof code ("Udon") is being merged into node software. — [ZecHub Vol.31](https://zechub.substack.com/p/zcash-shielded-news-vol31); [ePrint 2025/2031](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2031); [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/zcash-tachyon-udon-zakura-common-integration/)
- I found no Zcash SMS wallet. "Z-TEXT" is a BitcoinZ project, not Zcash. — [capital.com](https://capital.com/en-gb/news/text-zk-snarks-messenger-launches-no-phone-no-email-no-sim)
- I found no Zcash-over-Nostr payment project. — (search returned only generic wallet pages, e.g. [awesome-zcash-guide](https://github.com/iAnonymous3000/awesome-zcash-guide))
- Tangential: a ZCG application for a "Zcash Durable Payment Outbox" ($30,000; Rust service with SQLite persistence). Whether it covers offline use is unverified. — [ZCG GitHub #506](https://github.com/ZcashCommunityGrants/zcashcommunitygrants/issues/506)

### Inferences
- The project would be first-of-kind for ZEC. The closest Zcash-native primitives are PCZT for building and signing transactions on an offline device and moving the bytes elsewhere, FROST/ZIP 312 for co-signed escrow notes, and ZIP 221 for compact chain-state proofs.
- PCZT matters for the mesh. One party (the sender's device) can create and sign, a basecamp FROST co-signer can add its share, and a gateway can broadcast, each over separate hops. The roles are already separated in the format.
- ZIP 324 is Sapling-specific as drafted (Sapling derivation path and note). Using it for Ironwood would mean re-specifying derivation for Orchard/Ironwood keys. It is also explicitly not an offline primitive, because the recipient needs a chain lookup.
- nuttycom is likely Kris Nuttycombe, a ZIP 324 owner (handle-to-name mapping is my inference). If so, the ECC engineer most associated with bearer URIs has publicly ruled out offline-to-offline acceptance without trust.

### Gaps
- Ironwood **testnet** activation height/date and whether lightwalletd, Zebra and wallets (Zodl, Zkool, Zingo) support Ironwood on testnet were not found. This is for the Zcash-internals researcher.
- Whether ZIP 312/frost-client explicitly supports the Ironwood pool, and whether Ironwood keeps the Orchard key structure (`ask`/`ak`, `nk`, `rivk`, RedPallas spend auth), was not confirmed from a primary spec. ## 3. Architectural patterns: trust, double-spend window, finality, offline verifiability, privacy

### Takeaway
Distributed-systems theory (CAP) forces a choice. An offline payment system must either restrict the user's control of their device (trusted hardware), detect double-spenders afterward and punish them (identity or collateral), or route the check through an authority the recipient can reach (custodian, mint, or co-signer). Pure store-and-forward (pattern 1) has a double-spend window that runs until on-chain confirmation. Bearer instruments (pattern 2) have the same window plus interception risk, unless a mint or custodian checks them. Hardware (pattern 3) narrows the window only while the hardware holds, and one break allows unlimited double-spends. Local federations or co-signers (pattern 4) can give real local finality, because the spender cannot sign alone. A local chain copy (pattern 5) improves what can be verified but never closes the window. In a mesh setting "offline" usually means "no internet", not "no network". A basecamp authority reachable over the mesh turns the problem into an online check against a local authority, and that is the strongest design.

### Cited Findings

**Theory and general results**
- Grothoff and Dold (GNU Taler) argue that distributed systems cannot guarantee consistency and availability during a partition, so "any offline electronic payment system is left with one of the following choices": limit user control of the device (trusted hardware) or identify double-spenders after the fact. Trusted hardware can be broken by well-resourced attackers, who might even force network outages to enable multi-spending. Retroactive deanonymization conflicts with privacy, and accidental double-spends such as backup restores can void it. Losing the hardware means losing the cash. Their recommendation is an optional offline mode where "the payee decides whether to accept offline payments based on counterparty risk", with offline use "restricted to scenarios where it is actually required". — [Grothoff & Dold, "Why a Digital Euro should be Online-first and Bearer-based"](https://git.gnunet.org/taler/marketing/file/papers/2021-offline/offline.tex.html)
- IBM Research (2024): "the lack of connectivity during the payments renders double spending attacks trivial". Identifying and penalizing double spenders "may not be sufficient against concerted efforts by states or well-funded institutions". Their SE does only "deleting spent tokens and generating a signature" (ECDSA-equivalent cost), plus double-spender identification "on the off-chance an attacker successfully double spends". — [ePrint 2024/1746](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1746)
- Chaum–Fiat–Naor (Crypto '88) was the first off-line untraceable cash. Spending a coin once keeps the user anonymous, and spending it twice reveals their identity (detect-and-punish, no hardware). — [Naor abstract page](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~naor/PAPERS/untrace_abs.html); [Finney 1993 explainer](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/detecting-double-spending/)
- Non-hardware alternatives: Blaze–Ioannidis–Keromytis (FC 2001) offline micropayments use credentials and risk management with no trusted hardware. OVERDRAFT (TU Delft) uses reputation-weighted loan networks. — [Blaze et al.](https://www.mattblaze.org/papers/knpay.pdf); [arXiv 2504.05143](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05143v1)
- A March 2026 SoK of 36 payment designs lists offline payments as an open research gap. — [arXiv 2603.16320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16320)

**Pattern 1: sign or prove offline, store-and-forward to a gateway (TxTenna, Meshtastic bridges, Darkwire, SMS relays, txtenna-python)**
- Mechanism: the tx is fully signed offline, segmented, relayed hop by hop, and broadcast by any internet node. — [txTenna](https://github.com/remyers/txTenna); [Blockstream/goTenna](https://blog.blockstream.com/en-gotenna-satellite-api-integration); [Decrypt/Darkwire](https://decrypt.co/322192/project-send-bitcoin-without-internet-access)
- These projects provide no offline finality. Settlement happens only on-chain (see Section 1). ZIP 324's guidance of 10 confirmations before treating a payment as final is a Zcash datapoint. — [ZIP 324](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0324)

**Pattern 2: bearer value (ZIP 324, paper and brain wallets, ecash, channels)**
- ZIP 324: the sender keeps the key and can cancel by sweeping, and the race is resolved only by "MUST appropriately handle". Interception equals theft, the amount "may change (possibly to zero)" until finalization, and finalization needs a chain lookup. — [ZIP 324](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0324)
- HF 2019 demo: a bearer key or brain wallet was handed over the air, so anyone listening could race the recipient. — [Bitcoinist](https://bitcoinist.com/bitcoin-sent-ham-radio/amp/)
- Ecash: the mint alone detects double spends, at redemption. DLEQ proves authenticity offline but not unspentness. P2PK pre-locking to the recipient key is enforced by the mint, and a locktime refund path reopens the sender's spend after expiry. — [Delving Bitcoin](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/building-intuition-for-the-cashu-blind-signature-scheme/506/2); [NUT-11](https://cashubtc.github.io/nuts/11/). Mint trust: a dishonest operator could issue unbacked ecash. — [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/taking-digital-payments-offline)
- Lightning/channels: without a watchtower, the injured peer must "be online regularly and monitor every single transaction in every block for a breach before the arbitration time has elapsed". Watchtowers broadcast justice transactions for offline nodes, ideally run on a separate machine and network, and cannot see commitment contents. — [Lightning Labs docs](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/payment-channels/watchtowers); [BitMEX Research](https://www.bitmex.com/zh-hant/blog/lightning-network-part-4-all-adopt-the-watchtower)
- LNbits LNPoS can create invoices offline. The payment proof is a secret revealed once the invoice is paid, but someone online must still pay it. — [LNbits LNPoS](https://docs.lnbits.com/extensions/lnpos/)

**Pattern 3: hardware-enforced offline value (CBDC, secure elements)**
- BIS Project Polaris "Part 1: A handbook for offline payments with CBDC" (May 2023) covers three device options (SE-based, TEE-based, secure software-based) and offline risk management including transaction history and block lists. — [BIS othp64](https://www.bis.org/publ/othp64.pdf); [BIS Polaris](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc/polaris.htm)
- BIS/BoE Project Rosalind (June 2023) built 33 API endpoints and tested more than 30 use cases including offline. It found offline CBDC payments possible via APIs but with "a range of challenges". — [BIS Rosalind](https://www.bis.org/publ/othp69.htm)
- Digital euro: on 2 Oct 2025 the ECB ranked Giesecke+Devrient (with Nexi and Capgemini) first for the offline solution, and named equensWorldline second in an update on 15 Oct 2025. Value is stored on devices' secure elements (phones, cards). Issuance will be decided only after the Digital Euro Regulation is adopted. — [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/intro/news/html/ecb.mipnews251002.en.html); [Capgemini/G+D](https://www.capgemini.com/fr-fr/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2025/10/2025-10-01-EN_PI-CT-ECB-Digital-Euro-Offline-Solution_final.pdf)
- A critique of the digital euro offline design: "Fraud such as double-spending can only be detected with a delay during defunding". "A successful attacker can double-spend an unlimited amount of times". Liability is unclear. A holding limit of ~€3,000 is discussed. It cites SE breaches (laser fault-injection exploit chain on a secure element, Hériveaux 2022; EUCLEAK side channel on YubiKey 5, Roche 2024) and TEE/TPM breaches (TPM-FAIL 2020, SGAxe 2020, LVI 2020, CLKSCREW 2017, FaulTPM 2023, RMPocalypse 2025). It notes SE attacks mostly needed physical access, while TEEs fell to software-only attacks. — [arXiv 2601.18644](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18644v1)
- PayOff (ETH Zurich, 2024): "Proposed solutions based on secure elements offer no guarantees in case of compromise." It adds safeguards for SE failure, but offline storage and messages grow with unsynced payments. — [arXiv 2408.06956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06956)
- e-CNY: phone-based offline payments are capped at 500 RMB and 10 transactions, beyond which a password is entered on the receiver's device. A SIM-card hard wallet (China Mobile/Unicom/Telecom with ICBC) offers GP-spec secure key management and "dual offline" NFC. — [Finextra](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/41600/chinas-e-cny-app-launches-offline-payments); [Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/digital-rmb-yuan-cbdc-sim-card-wallet-offline-payments/); [China Daily HK](https://chinadailyhk.com/article/238110). Chinese academic literature flags "unclear settlement finality" and "double payment such as wallet data tampering or delayed repeated payment" for offline e-CNY. — [SUFE journal](https://qks.sufe.edu.cn/J/ArticleQuery/a2c0b82a-c231-4a50-8862-1608501ba05a)

**Pattern 4: local consensus, federation or co-signers**
- Fedimint spreads custody across guardians using BFT consensus. Guardians check double-spends at redemption and sign on-chain withdrawals. — [spark.money Fedimint](https://www.spark.money/research/fedimint-federated-ecash)
- Machankura is effectively a single custodian keeping an internal Lightning ledger, reachable over USSD without internet. — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/04/machankuras-solution-for-crypto-transactions/)
- FROST threshold spend authorization exists for Zcash shielded pools (ZIP 312, with ZF tooling audited). Only `ask` is shared, and a coordinator or signers see the transaction. — [ZIP 312](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0312); [ZF FROST book](https://frost.zfnd.org/zcash/technical-details.html)

**Pattern 5: local node with relayed chain copy**
- Blockstream Satellite and txtenna-python let an off-grid full node validate the chain and see confirmations without uplink. It is Bitcoin-only and receive-only. — [Blockstream blog](https://blog.blockstream.com/en-gotenna-satellite-api-integration); [GitHub releases](https://github.com/Blockstream/satellite/releases)
- Zcash headers are 1,487 bytes each because of Equihash. FlyClient (ZIP 221) cuts the number of headers needed to logarithmic, but there is no nullifier-set commitment. — [Zebra](https://doc-internal.zebra.zfnd.org/zebra_chain/block/header/constant.BLOCK_HEADER_LENGTH.html); [ZIP 221](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0221)
- "LoRa mesh cannot sync the blockchain." — [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)

- **Double-spend mechanics in pattern 1 for Zcash (inference).** The sender builds tx₁ (note N → R) and hands it to the mesh. The sender can also build tx₂ (note N → self or a third party) with the same nullifier. Consensus accepts whichever is mined first and rejects the other as a duplicate nullifier. The recipient learns which one won only from chain state. Mitigation in pure pattern 1 is limited to counterparty trust, collateral or reputation, credit limits, and keeping value small.
- **Detect-and-punish is weaker on Zcash than on Chaum-style ecash (inference).** Two conflicting Zcash spends of the same note do expose the same nullifier, so the loser holds cryptographic evidence that some holder of that note's spend authority signed two spends. But the spend-auth signatures are under rerandomized keys (ZIP 312 / Orchard design), so the evidence does not identify the sender unless identity was bound out of band. Example: the sender signs an attestation "I, Logos-ID X, spend nullifier nf to R" with a long-term key. That attestation becomes the basis for reputation or collateral slashing.
- **Hardware for Zcash (inference).** A Zcash SE would need to (a) generate `ask` inside the element and never export it, (b) keep a monotonic spent-set of nullifiers it has signed for, and (c) attest to that policy. Keystone and Ledger do (a) only partially (seed-derived, seed backup exists) and do not do (b). With a seed backup, the user can restore on a second device and double-spend, which is exactly the backup conflict Grothoff and Dold describe. Any SE break allows unlimited double-spends, as with the digital euro critique.
- **The key reframing for a basecamp mesh (inference).** Logos mesh connectivity between basecamps means the recipient can often reach some authority in real time, just not the Zcash network. Patterns 2b, 4a and 4b then give near-instant local finality, because the double-spend check runs against a local authority over the mesh. Patterns 1, 2a and 5 do not. That is the main architectural lever.
- **Quorum design for a multi-basecamp federation (inference).** With a single FROST threshold t-of-n over {S, G1…Gk}, "S plus any one guardian" lets two different guardians each co-sign a different spend of the same note. That re-creates double-spending unless the guardians reach consensus first. Two fixes. (a) Require t such that any two signing quorums share an honest guardian (BFT-style intersection), which needs guardian-to-guardian messaging over the mesh at payment time. (b) Pin each escrow note to one designated co-signer basecamp. Option (b) is simpler and lower-latency but makes that basecamp a single point of liveness.
- **Timing hazards for store-and-forward on Zcash (inference, verify with Zcash-internals researcher).**
  - Zcash transactions carry an expiry height (ZIP 203), so a tx relayed slower than its expiry window becomes unminable. The builder should set expiry generously or disable it.
  - Shielded spends reference an anchor (a historical note-commitment root). An offline sender with a stale but valid view can still build valid spends, provided it holds witnesses for its notes.

## 4. Can a recipient with no chain access be cryptographically sure a shielded Zcash note is unspent? Best-known compromises

### Takeaway
No. Confirmed by reasoning from the protocol and by every source that addresses it. "Unspent" means "this note's nullifier is not in the current global nullifier set, and the holder of spend authority hasn't published or won't publish a spend elsewhere first". Neither fact can be proven from local data during a partition. Zcash block headers don't even commit to the nullifier set (ZIP 221), so a stale non-membership proof isn't natively available either. Every workable design substitutes trust: in a co-signer or federation, in hardware, in a custodian or mint, or in collateral, credit limits and reputation, or it waits for on-chain finality. The strongest compromise for basecamps is a FROST-controlled escrow note (2-of-2 {sender, basecamp} or a quorum of basecamps). The spender then cannot sign alone, and the co-signer's spent-registry is authoritative because no spend of that note can exist without its share.

### Cited Findings
- Offline double-spending is "trivial" without connectivity, and prevention requires secure hardware. Detect-and-punish isn't enough against well-funded attackers. — [IBM, ePrint 2024/1746](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1746)
- Under partition, a payment system must either restrict device control or identify double-spenders afterwards. Neither is a cryptographic guarantee of unspentness. — [Grothoff & Dold](https://git.gnunet.org/taler/marketing/file/papers/2021-offline/offline.tex.html)
- Zcash core engineers say offline-to-offline acceptance is inherently vulnerable to double-spending, and the recipient bears the risk because a malicious wallet can't be prevented. — [Zcash forum, nuttycom/aprojection/james_katz](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/how-to-pay-offline-with-zcash-in-any-situation/50167)
- ZIP 324's recipient flow requires a "blockchain lookup" to determine that notes exist and are unspent, and forbids presenting the payment as final before confirmations. — [ZIP 324](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0324)
- Zcash header commitments (FlyClient MMR) include note commitment tree roots, but a nullifier-set commitment was considered and not included. — [ZIP 221](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0221)
- Nullifier sets grow without bound and every validator must store them. This motivates Tachyon's redesign. — [Bowe & Miers, ePrint 2025/2031](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2031)
- In ecash, the only party who can tell a double spend is the mint. DLEQ proves validity, not unspentness. — [Delving Bitcoin](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/building-intuition-for-the-cashu-blind-signature-scheme/506/2)
- FROST for Zcash splits only the spend authorization key. Approval requires `MIN_PARTICIPANTS`, signers must verify the SIGHASH, and the coordinator and signers learn the transaction. Orchard FROST wallets share the full viewing key (`ak`, `nk`, `rivk`) among participants. — [ZIP 312](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0312); [ZF FROST technical details](https://frost.zfnd.org/zcash/technical-details.html)
- Cashu's analogous construction, P2PK-locked tokens with `n_sigs` multisig, makes spending conditional on specific keys and is enforced by the mint. A locktime refund path re-opens spending after expiry. — [NUT-11](https://cashubtc.github.io/nuts/11/)

### Inferences
- **Why it's impossible, three independent reasons:**
  1. *Present-state problem.* Even a perfect, signed snapshot of the nullifier set at time T says nothing about spends broadcast after T on another side of the partition. That is the CAP argument.
  2. *No commitment to prove against.* Zcash headers commit to note-commitment roots, which let you prove a note *exists*, but not to the nullifier set. So even a stale "not spent as of block H" proof would need a trusted full node's word, or a Tachyon-era PCD proof if that ships.
  3. *Sender capability.* Whoever holds `ask` (or the ZIP 324 URI key) can produce a second valid spend at any time, and consensus deduplicates nullifiers only at mining.
- **What an offline recipient *can* verify, without trust, from a signed Ironwood tx relayed over the mesh:**
  - its proofs and signatures (stateless checks);
  - that an output decrypts with their IVK, with the stated amount and memo;
  - that the tx is internally balanced.
  With relayed headers (pattern 5) they can also check that the anchor is a real historical root. They cannot check the nullifiers.
- **Best compromises, strongest first, for the basecamp setting:**
  1. **FROST escrow note, 2-of-2 {S, B}.**
     - *Setup.* While connected, S funds an Ironwood note whose spend authority is FROST-shared between S and basecamp B. B can check on-chain that it exists and is confirmed. Since no spend is possible without B's share, B's local registry is the complete truth about that note even when fully offline.
     - *Payment.* S proposes a PCZT spending the escrow note to R. B checks the SIGHASH and the nullifier against its registry, records the nullifier, co-signs, and sends R the completed tx plus a B-signed attestation (nullifier, tx id, amount, recipient commitment). R trusts B, not S.
     - *Settlement.* The tx is store-and-forwarded to any gateway and mined later.
     - *Residual risks:*
       - S+B collusion, mitigated by reputation, B's collateral, and value limits;
       - B unavailability. Funds are stuck because Zcash shielded notes have no timelocked refund path; a pre-signed refund would be immediately spendable by S, re-opening the double-spend;
       - B registry durability and rollback, which calls for an append-only log, possibly replicated to a peer basecamp;
       - privacy: B (and any coordinator) knows the escrow note's FVK, so B sees amounts and recipients;
       - interactive FROST rounds over the mesh: 2 rounds, plus a confidential channel for the randomizer.
  2. **FROST quorum across basecamps (t-of-n guardians, with or without S's share).** This removes the single B, but needs intersecting quorums and guardian-to-guardian coordination before signing (see Section 3). Without S's share it is custody by the federation, Fedimint-style.
  3. **Basecamp-issued ecash or ledger backed by ZEC** (Cashu/Fedimint/Machankura analogue). Instant if the mint is reachable over the mesh, and blind signatures hide payer and payee from the mint. It is custodial and adds a non-Zcash trust layer.
  4. **Credit or collateral with limits.** Accept un-co-signed pattern-1 txs up to a per-counterparty limit, backed by a deposit (itself a FROST escrow) or reputation (OVERDRAFT/Blaze style), with an identity-binding attestation so a double-spend becomes slashable evidence.
  5. **Hardware.** An SE-held, non-exportable `ask` with a monotonic nullifier log and attestation. It is the only option that works with zero connectivity to any authority. Every CBDC-grade analysis says one break means unlimited double-spends. It also conflicts with seed backup, and no Zcash SE applet with spend-once policy exists (as far as I found).
  6. **Bearer URI (ZIP 324 style).** Acceptable only between parties who already trust each other or for low value, since the sender can always reclaim.

### Gaps
- No academic paper specifically on offline double-spend prevention for *shielded* UTXO/nullifier systems (Zcash or Monero) was found.
- No existing implementation or spec of a "co-signer spent-registry" escrow for Zcash was found. This appears novel and would need its own security review.
- Whether Tachyon (if deployed) will offer compact, verifiable "unspent as of block H" proofs a recipient could check against relayed headers is unclear from the sources seen. Even if it does, they remain stale by the relay latency.

## 5. Applicability assessment for an Ironwood-only testnet ZEC transfer over Logos mesh

### Takeaway
Use pattern 1 (offline-signed Ironwood tx, store-and-forward over Logos to any basecamp gateway with backhaul) as the settlement path. Layer pattern 4b (FROST 2-of-2 escrow notes with a basecamp co-signer) on top when the recipient needs to accept before on-chain confirmation. Add a light pattern 5 (relayed headers or FlyClient proofs plus mined-tx receipts back over the mesh) so recipients can eventually verify settlement. Ecash or custodial ledgers work but add a non-Zcash trust layer. Hardware enforcement doesn't exist for Zcash and has known breaks. Lightning-style channels are not available in the shielded pool. ZIP 324-style bearer URIs suit only trusted or low-value transfers, and their interception risk is amplified on a multi-hop mesh.

### Cited Findings
- Ironwood reuses the fixed Orchard circuit and Halo 2, with Orchard withdraw-only. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/409934/zcash-ironwood-upgrade-launching-new-shielded-pool-after-orchard-vulnerability)
- FROST spend-authorization multisig is specified for the Orchard (Pallas) ciphersuite with re-randomization. ZF tooling (frostd, frost-client) is audited, and a wallet (Zkool) ships FROST shielded multisig. — [ZIP 312](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0312); [Least Authority](https://leastauthority.com/blog/zcash-frost-demo/); [zkool2 wiki](https://github.com/hhanh00/zkool2/wiki)
- PCZT separates creation, proving and signing, and is already used for air-gapped and hardware signing (Keystone, Ledger). — [docs.rs pczt](https://docs.rs/pczt/); [Ledger](https://developers.ledger.com/docs/device-interaction/dmk-ts/references/signers/zcash)
- Zcash headers are 1,487 bytes, and FlyClient gives logarithmic chain verification without a nullifier commitment. — [Zebra](https://doc-internal.zebra.zfnd.org/zebra_chain/block/header/constant.BLOCK_HEADER_LENGTH.html); [ZIP 221](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0221)
- Meshtastic payload buffer is 228 bytes and LongFast is ~1.07 kbps. A Bitcoin tx already needs manual segmentation. — [BTCtoolshed bridge](https://github.com/BTCtoolshed/MeshtasticBitcoinCore_Bridge); [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-satellite-node-comparison)
- ZIP 324 is Draft, Sapling-based, requires a chain lookup to accept, and leaves the sender able to cancel. — [ZIP 324](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0324)
- Lightning safety depends on monitoring the chain within a dispute window, or delegating to watchtowers. — [Lightning Labs](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/payment-channels/watchtowers)

### Inferences

**Applicability matrix (inference):**

| Pattern | Works with Ironwood shielded today? | Notes for this design |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Offline-signed tx plus store-and-forward | **Yes** | Build with PCZT. Set expiry so mesh latency can't strand it. Send the full tx to R as an advance "receipt" (R decrypts its output). Any basecamp with backhaul broadcasts. Return a mined-at-height receipt to S and R. Gateways see only encrypted tx bytes, but mesh metadata (who sent what to the gateway, when) is the main privacy leak. |
| 2a. ZIP 324-style bearer note | Partially (needs Ironwood re-spec; recipient can't verify offline) | Only for trusted or low-value transfers. On a multi-hop mesh, every relay that can read the payload could steal, so it must be end-to-end encrypted to R. The sender can always reclaim. |
| 2b. Ecash backed by ZEC | Yes, as an overlay (custodial) | Mint is a basecamp or federation reachable over the mesh. Strong payer/payee privacy versus the mint. Not "Zcash shielded" in itself; peg-in and peg-out happen on Ironwood. |
| 2c. Lightning or payment channels | **No** | The shielded pool has no script, timelock or HTLC capability to enforce penalties or refunds (background knowledge, not sourced here; confirm with Zcash-internals researcher). A Zcash Lightning implementation is not known to exist. |
| 3. Hardware-enforced spend-once | Not available | It would require a custom SE applet holding a non-exportable `ask` plus a nullifier log plus attestation. It inherits all CBDC SE risks. Possible future research, not a near-term building block. |
| 4a. Basecamp custodial ledger | Yes (trust-heavy) | Simple, but the basecamp is custodian, sees everything, and is a single point of failure. |
| 4b. FROST escrow note with basecamp co-signer | **Yes (best fit)** | Gives local finality without trusting the sender. Costs: pre-funding while connected, B liveness (no timelock refund), FVK disclosure to co-signers, 2-round FROST plus a confidential randomizer channel over Logos. A 2-of-3 setup with two basecamps needs intersecting quorums or note pinning. |
| 5. Relayed chain state | Partially | Full compact-block relay is too heavy for LoRa-class links (inference: ~1,152 headers/day × 1,487 B ≈ 1.7 MB/day of headers alone, assuming 75 s blocks). FlyClient proofs or periodic signed checkpoints plus per-tx inclusion receipts are feasible. They confirm *settled* payments but never pre-confirmation unspentness. |

- **Suggested composite flow (inference):**
  1. While connected, S deposits into a FROST {S, B} Ironwood escrow note and waits for confirmations. B records the note as "live".
  2. Offline, S builds a PCZT paying R. B verifies it and checks its registry, co-signs, and returns an attestation to R over Logos. R treats the payment as "locally final, B-guaranteed".
  3. Either party pushes the tx through Logos store-and-forward to any gateway basecamp, which broadcasts.
  4. The gateway (or a satellite or radio header feed) returns an inclusion receipt with header or FlyClient proof. R upgrades the status to "on-chain final" after N confirmations.
  5. Disputes: B's attestation plus S's identity-bound intent signature make collusion or double-spend attempts attributable.
- **Testnet-specific notes (inference):**
  - Testnet ZEC has no value, so collateral and credit mechanisms can't be economically tested. Simulate them.
  - Testnet chain reorgs and low hash-rate mean confirmation thresholds should be higher than mainnet intuition.
  - Ironwood testnet activation and wallet or lightwalletd support must be confirmed first (see Gaps).
- **Privacy ranking (inference):** pattern 1 leaks the least to co-signers (none) but leaks mesh metadata. Pattern 4b leaks note contents to B by construction. Pattern 2b hides payer and payee from the mint but adds custody. A deployment could let users choose per payment: wait for chain (private, slow) or get B-guaranteed (fast, B sees it).

### Gaps
- Ironwood testnet activation height and current testnet support in Zebra, lightwalletd, Zodl, Zkool and Zingo were not confirmed.
- Ironwood transaction sizes (bytes per action and proof size) were not sourced here. They determine how many Logos/LoRa messages a payment needs and belong to the Zcash-internals and radio researchers.
- I found no source on whether ZIP 312/frost-client already targets Ironwood (versus Orchard) key formats.
- The claim that shielded Zcash lacks the script and timelock features needed for Lightning-style channels and timelocked refunds is background knowledge and should be confirmed from the protocol spec by the Zcash-internals researcher.
- No measured data exists on FROST signing round-trip success over lossy, high-latency mesh links. It would need prototyping.
